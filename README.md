# TAT — Project Lifecycle

**Group coursework — MSc Project Management, Finance & Risk, City, University of London**

A planning and quantitative risk analysis exercise for a sub-project of a hypothetical Trans-Atlantic Tunnel ("TAT") construction programme. The deliverable focused on resource management for a single iteration covering excavation and lining operations, applying standard PMO methods end-to-end: scope and stakeholder definition, work breakdown, network modelling, critical path identification, PERT estimation, and probabilistic completion analysis.

---

## What this project demonstrates

- **Work Breakdown Structure** — five phases decomposed into 69 activities with dependencies, durations, and ownership
- **PERT analysis** — three-point estimation (optimistic, most likely, pessimistic) for every activity, expected time and variance calculation, critical path identification
- **Probabilistic completion modelling** — Z-score analysis for 90% confidence completion (496 days), probability of finishing 15% early (9.85%), and crashed-duration analysis after 10% reduction (453 days at 90% confidence)
- **Monte Carlo simulation** — distribution of completion times to validate the analytical PERT result
- **Risk register** — 20 risks with likelihood, impact, severity, mitigation strategy, and risk ownership across all project phases
- **Stakeholder mapping** — internal and external stakeholder analysis across delivery, regulatory, and community dimensions
- **Network diagram and Gantt chart** — full precedence logic visualisation with critical activities highlighted

---

## Methodology notes

The team adopted an Agile-inspired framing for the larger TAT programme, with the principle that the bigger tunnel construction would be broken into iterations. This deck represents one iteration's plan — a deliberate scoping choice to keep the analysis tractable while reflecting how iterative delivery applies in heavy-construction contexts where physical dependencies are more rigid than in software work.
